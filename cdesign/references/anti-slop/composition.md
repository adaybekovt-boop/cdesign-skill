# Composition critique

Load when page structure, hero arrangement, grids, cards, or section rhythm carry the direction.

Provenance: the recurrence claims below are heuristic/internal pattern research.

## DEFAULT REJECT

### C-01 — Generic centered stack

Headline, deck, CTA pair, and decorative image form one centered column with no project-specific object, field, rail, or content relationship. A poster, title card, manifesto, or interaction gate may justify this composition; “clean” does not.

### C-02 — Copy-left / device-right template

A near-even split places generic copy beside a laptop, phone, calendar, or dashboard whose contents do not demonstrate a real task. Real product UI can pass when crop, scale, and placement explain a specific workflow.

### C-03 — Equal bento without hierarchy

Cells share radius, padding, icon scale, and visual weight even though the content has unequal importance. Bento is allowed when cell size, order, or behavior maps to real priority.

### C-04 — Infinite card world

Six or more modules share the same rounded container and internal icon-title-copy structure, or cards are nested inside a rounded section-card. A card-based product surface is an exception; the marketing page around it should not also become cards.

### C-05 — Template section train

Hero → logos → three features → stats → testimonials → pricing → FAQ → CTA appears as repeated full-width bands with the same measure and padding. Keep only rooms supported by the brief and vary density according to their jobs.

### C-06 — Uniform shell and rhythm

Every section uses the same centered max-width, vertical padding, split ratio, and density. Equality can be the motif of a catalog or manual, but it must be named in the genome.

### C-07 — Decorative composition objects

Floating cards, abstract props, browser chrome, or empty oversized padding substitute for an anchor. They pass only when tied to a real product, supplied reference, brand geometry, or interaction.

## WATCHLIST

- repeated 60/40 asymmetry across every section;
- full-viewport hero with no second-screen reason;
- decorative grid overlay unrelated to alignment or data;
- sticky side index that duplicates rather than navigates;
- testimonial or pricing cards with equal height and proof weight;
- five-column footer on a short page;
- large empty zones whose only reason is “premium spacing.”

## Stack signals

Count the patterns individually, then inspect combinations such as:

- centered hero + badge + three icon cards + logo row;
- fake dashboard + floating cards + uniform section padding;
- max-width shell + equal bento + rounded containers everywhere.

One ingredient may be harmless. A stack without a shared reason is a redesign signal.

If animation creates temporary hierarchy because the still frame has no anchor, load `motion.md` and score its authoritative `M-01` once. Do not score a second composition rule for the same problem.

## Critique prompts

1. What is the first-viewport anchor, and why is it specific to this project?
2. Which section changes the page’s density or reading mode for a real reason?
3. Could any block be removed without losing meaning, action, or identity?
4. Would the composition remain legible and recognizable in a still screenshot?
