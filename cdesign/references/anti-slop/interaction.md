# Interaction critique

Load for custom cursor, magnetic, drag, hover, sound, command palette, spatial navigation, or gesture-led directions.

Provenance: recurrence and coding-agent-default claims are heuristic/internal pattern research.

## DEFAULT REJECT

### I-01 — Document-wide cursor costume

A follower replaces or decorates the pointer across a reading or commerce page without acting as a lens, brush, target, inspector, or local tool. An experimental surface may justify a precise custom pointer within that surface.

### I-02 — Magnetism everywhere

Many buttons, cards, and navigation items pull toward the pointer. One restrained control can pass when attraction supports its meaning and coarse pointers receive a normal target.

### I-03 — Whole-grid hover theater

Hovering one card dims, blurs, scales, or relayouts all siblings. A small mutually exclusive selector may justify a quiet sibling response.

### I-04 — Repeated card tilt or shine

Information cards use pointer-bound perspective and reflective overlays as a generic premium signal. A physical collectible or material product may justify one such object.

### I-05 — Follower labels on every tile

Generic “View / Open / Drag” pills chase the pointer while captions are absent from the composition. A single gallery can use a specific local label if keyboard and touch equivalents exist.

### I-06 — Hover media wall

Many tiles autoplay video or swap imagery on hover. A real showreel may allow one playing item at a time with explicit mobile behavior.

### I-07 — Horizontal drag/pin as decoration

A row of ordinary feature cards hijacks vertical scroll or requires drag merely to look interactive. Sequential work, timelines, film strips, or instruments can justify the axis with an accessible fallback.

### I-08 — Decorative command palette or sound

A small marketing page adds command search that duplicates navigation, or audio begins without an explicit opt-in/mute model. Tools, catalogs, documentation, and sound-led projects are valid exceptions.

## WATCHLIST

- one small-travel magnetic CTA;
- button press squash;
- tooltip on an icon-only control;
- custom scrollbar that preserves visibility and contrast;
- hover underline or color;
- drag enhancement with visible controls;
- pointer-as-lens with a designed touch rest state.

## Delivery boundary

Hidden primary controls, hover-only required content, missing keyboard access, invisible focus, gesture-only navigation, coarse-pointer cursor effects, and inaccessible sound controls are QA failures, not Anti-Slop `HARD BAN` rules. Fix them regardless of score.

## Critique prompts

1. What task becomes clearer because of this interaction?
2. Is the interaction local to the object it manipulates?
3. Can keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, and no-audio users complete the same task?
4. Could the interaction be removed without changing product recognition? If yes, why keep it?
