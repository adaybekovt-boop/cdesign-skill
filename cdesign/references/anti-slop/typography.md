# Typography critique

Load when typography carries identity or when review shows weak hierarchy, default pairing, or decorative type effects.

Provenance: font-frequency and coding-agent-default claims are heuristic/internal pattern research.

## DEFAULT REJECT

### T-01 — Undecided default stack

A common neutral sans is used for display and body with no optical, width, role, scale, or structural distinction. The font name alone never fails; unexamined use does. A documented brand face or product UI can justify a neutral family.

### T-02 — Replacement-default pairing

A fashionable grotesk or serif accent is chosen only to avoid an older default, while the page keeps the same extrabold headline, crushed tracking, tiny labels, and neutral body. Require a reason tied to voice, measure, script support, or content density.

### T-03 — Serif garnish

One italic serif word is inserted inside a sans headline to simulate editorial or luxury direction, but no caption, deck, measure, crop, or reading system supports that role. A real serif system may use roman and italic across meaningful roles.

### T-04 — Flat or arbitrary scale

Headline, section title, body, and metadata have no clear hierarchy, or many one-off sizes replace a small role system. Extreme sameness can be deliberate in a poster or dense tool; otherwise define the hierarchy in the genome.

### T-05 — Giant vague headline

A long abstract slogan is set at display scale and wraps repeatedly. Large type passes when wording is specific and the crop, line breaks, or collision are part of the concept.

### T-06 — Tracking as personality

Crushed display tracking, wide body tracking, or tracked uppercase labels appear automatically rather than serving a typeface or information role.

### T-07 — Broken measure

Body copy stretches too wide, centered paragraphs become hard to scan, or decks have no distinct measure. Exact character counts are guidance, not a universal formula; judge language, typeface, and viewport.

### T-08 — Mono as pseudo-technical costume

Monospace is used for decorative eyebrows and section labels without code, identifiers, tabular data, or a brand reason.

### T-09 — Type effects as identity substitute

Gradient-clipped headings or identical split-character reveals recur across headings without meaning. A single kinetic or chromatic type mechanism can pass when it is the project-specific piece.

## WATCHLIST

- starter pairing survives despite contradictory brief or reference;
- four or more weights without defined roles;
- one leading value across display and body;
- all-caps controls that weaken scanning;
- giant text used as wallpaper without crop or collision;
- pure white on pure black chosen by default;
- tiny gray body that technically passes contrast but still reads as intentionally receded.

## Accessibility boundary

Text contrast below the project’s accessibility target is a QA failure, not an AI-slop `HARD BAN`. Fix it regardless of score. The same applies to clipping, unreadable line breaks, missing language glyphs, and font-loading failures.

## Critique prompts

1. What job does each type role perform?
2. Which type decision could only belong to this product or content?
3. Would replacing the system with a neutral sans and tight tracking erase the identity?
4. Do the scale and measure survive the target language and mobile viewport?
