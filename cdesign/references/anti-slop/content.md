# Content, proof, and copy critique

Load whenever copy, proof, pricing, testimonials, claims, FAQs, or calls to action are created or restructured.

The authoritative fabrication `HARD BAN` rules are in [INDEX.md](INDEX.md). Do not repeat or rescore them here.

Provenance: recurrence and generated-copy-pattern claims are heuristic/internal pattern research.

## DEFAULT REJECT

### K-01 — Mad-lib headline

“AI-powered X for modern Y,” “the future of Z,” “X that just works,” or an equivalent category costume names no outcome, object, or constraint. Pair a necessary category line with a concrete descriptor.

### K-02 — Interchangeable benefits

Three sibling blocks promise saving time, working smarter, scaling, elevating, or unlocking without naming a workflow, artifact, capability, or limit.

### K-03 — Generic dual CTA

“Get started” plus “Learn more,” or equivalent vague labels, appear without a real destination. Use the next action and name what opens.

### K-04 — Section-label cosplay

Tracked uppercase labels, decorative chapter numbers, or generic headings such as “Why us” and “The difference” add style but no orientation. Real indexes and long-form chapters can justify numbering.

### K-05 — Proof-shaped leftovers

Ratings, “Loved by,” “Featured in,” integration strips, comparison tables, or beta/new pills imply evidence that the brief does not provide. Fabricated instances trigger the core truth gate.

### K-06 — Template FAQ

An accordion invents objections and answers solely because a landing-page template expects it. Keep real user questions, regulated explanations, or product-specific constraints.

### K-07 — Language drift

Visible copy mixes template English with the brief’s primary language or translates inconsistently. Brand names and explicitly bilingual products are exceptions.

### K-08 — Empty marketing language

Abstract verbs and claims replace a concrete object or outcome: unlock, elevate, unleash, empower, revolutionize, seamless, best-in-class, game-changing, “powered by AI,” “chaos into clarity,” and equivalent Russian phrases such as “раскройте потенциал,” “мир возможностей,” “новая эра,” “премиальный,” or “внимание к деталям.” Literal, quoted, verified proper-name, or necessary technical uses can pass; unsupported marketing use cannot.

## WATCHLIST

- eyebrow above every headline;
- “powered by AI” used as the identity rather than mechanism;
- generic three-step process;
- repeated CTA with no new context;
- empty mission/manifesto copy;
- pricing-card pattern when prices or plans are not confirmed;
- integration or competitor framing without verified names;
- changelog/newness chrome without real release information.

## Combination signals

- mad-lib headline + generic three-card benefits + logo row + dual CTA + FAQ;
- stat tiles + generic testimonial cards + awards/badges;
- section-number labels + vague manifesto + repeated “Get started.”

## Critique prompts

1. Which sentence could only describe this project?
2. Which names, numbers, and claims came from supplied evidence?
3. What real proof remains when proof-shaped decoration is removed?
4. Does every CTA name a destination or task that actually exists?
