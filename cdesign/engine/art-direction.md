# Art Direction Engine

Generate a visual thesis from evidence. Do not choose a named aesthetic.

## Inputs

Collect only what changes the design:

- product or institution truth
- audience and primary action
- content shape: sparse, narrative, catalog, data-heavy, procedural
- supplied imagery, product, logo, model, or existing interface
- reference anchors
- emotional target expressed as observable behavior
- device, accessibility, performance, and delivery constraints
- previous fingerprints when available

The business category may shape navigation, claims, and content priority. It must not map directly to a palette, font pairing, layout, or motion style. A strong supplied reference overrides category stereotypes unless it conflicts with usability or truth.

## Synthesis

Before coding, form one to three short candidate genomes according to ambiguity and project risk. When comparing multiple candidates, they must differ structurally, not by color swap. Vary at least six of these between candidates: hero composition, navigation, typography structure, dominant geometry, section rhythm, imagery treatment, motion mechanism, section sequence, signature motif.

Choose one candidate by answering:

1. Which candidate makes the product or content easiest to recognize?
2. Which candidate uses the supplied assets rather than decorating around them?
3. Which candidate creates the strongest specific signature with the least arbitrary machinery?
4. Which candidate survives mobile recomposition?
5. Which candidate is least similar to available fingerprints?

Do not keep candidate names such as “editorial fintech” or “luxury brutalism” as the output. Record concrete properties in the DESIGN_GENOME.

## Deliberate property combination

Do not blindly combine complete aesthetics. Combine compatible design properties deliberately.

For each property, write:

- **decision** — what will be visibly built
- **reason** — the project fact it serves
- **expression** — where it appears
- **limit** — what prevents it becoming a gimmick

Example:

> **geometry** — route-like polylines divide sections and connect real delivery stages.  
> **reason** — the product is a logistics network, so paths explain relationships.  
> **expression** — hero boundary, process transitions, and active navigation state.  
> **limit** — no decorative map labels or unrelated coordinate overlays.

Compatibility comes from a shared reason, rhythm, or geometry. Institutional composition, editorial typography, cartographic geometry, and mechanical motion can coexist when all four express traceable process. They fail when each is included as a separate fashionable effect.

## Coherence test

A direction passes when:

- one sentence can explain its visual logic without style adjectives;
- the signature motif recurs with changing function, not copy-paste decoration;
- typography, geometry, imagery, and motion agree on pace and hierarchy;
- removal of the signature decision would materially change the site;
- each visible flourish has a product, content, reference, or interaction reason.

If these are false, regenerate the genome before implementation.
